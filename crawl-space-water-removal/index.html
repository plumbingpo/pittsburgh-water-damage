Crawl Space Water Removal in Pittsburgh

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • White or dark fuzzy growth on joists, beams or the subfloor

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

What Happens if You Wait

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Settling Under the House

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Rot You Cannot See

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.
