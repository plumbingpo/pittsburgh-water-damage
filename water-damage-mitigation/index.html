Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. Our Pittsburgh crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Done well, it shrinks the repair that follows and sometimes removes the need for one.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. In fact there are two separate stages. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

Why Water Damage Mitigation Is Not a DIY Job

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Does the cabinet toe kick need to come off? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Under most policies you must act reasonably to keep the damage from growing, with proof that you did. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • Slope the soil away from the house so rainwater does not pool against the walls.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • A main shutoff that will not turn is no help, so locate and test yours.
  • Leave the heat on during winter trips so the plumbing cannot freeze.

How Our Water Damage Mitigation Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Pittsburgh Restoration Co:

1

Stop the Water Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Set Up Containment

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Each visit includes logged moisture readings, and the structure is usually ready for repair work within 3 to 5 days.

When to Call for Water Damage Mitigation in Pittsburgh

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.
  • Water is moving from one room into the next along the flooring.
  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.
  • The insurance company instructed you to take reasonable action against further damage.
  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.
  • Since the leak you have noticed puffed-up baseboards or gaps opening behind them.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
