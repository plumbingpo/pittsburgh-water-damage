Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Pittsburgh

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

For homeowners in Pittsburgh, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.

What Happens if You Wait

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

The ceiling under an upper floor laundry catches whatever water makes it past the floor above. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.

Wall Bases and Trim

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Cabinet Damage

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.
  • Spread of the water into other rooms, and into the ceiling beneath an upstairs laundry if there is one.
  • Presence of mold, which adds removal work in front of the drying.
  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
