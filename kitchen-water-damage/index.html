Kitchen Water Damage: What Pittsburgh Homeowners Should Know

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. We help Pittsburgh homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

Behind the Dishwasher

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.
