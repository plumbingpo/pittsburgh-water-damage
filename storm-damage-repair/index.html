Why Storm Damage Repair Is Not a DIY Job

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. What seems like a small leak indoors can hide widespread water damage in wall cavities, roof decking and insulation, which turns into mold and structural decay unless it is identified and dried properly. With moisture meters and thermal cameras, trained technicians follow the route water took and find all the areas it reached.

Emergency board-up and tarping also take proper technique to be effective. If a tarp is fastened poorly, it can direct water where you do not want it or give out when the wind picks up. Experienced in storm mitigation, our team picks appropriate methods and materials to keep the building protected until permanent repair work can be scheduled.

Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • For insurance purposes, keep a yearly set of pictures showing the state of your property
  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
  • Learn where the main water shutoff is and how to close it if pipes are damaged in a storm
  • Cracked caulk lets blown rain in, so inspect and renew the seals on doors and windows
  • Clean out downspouts and gutters regularly, which lets heavy rainfall drain away as it should
  • Extend downspouts and slope the soil so runoff moves away from the foundation

Signs You Need Storm Damage Repair in Pittsburgh

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Ceilings or walls that stain while it is storming or soon afterward
  • Rain blown in around door frames, window frames or openings in the wall
  • Attic or wall insulation that is damp or matted down following a storm
  • Ceiling drywall that sags or bulges, or paint that bubbles after rain
  • Damp, earthy odors that appear a few days after the storm
  • Sagging or detached gutters that spill rainwater down the siding
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
