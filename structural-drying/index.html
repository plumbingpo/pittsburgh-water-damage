No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Pittsburgh Restoration Co are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Pittsburgh

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Our Structural Drying Process

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

Final readings are documented, the equipment comes out and your property is certified at dry standard as soon as all affected materials test at acceptable levels.
