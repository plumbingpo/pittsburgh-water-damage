Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Pittsburgh Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. Sagging changes the picture. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

What It Looks Like

  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • Drywall that sags, bows or feels soft when pressed gently
  • Water trails on the upper wall, starting at the ceiling corner
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

Our Process

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Electricity Overhead

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. Water in a fixture can short the circuit, so switch off the breaker for that room.

Sudden Collapse

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. A bulge can drop a whole section onto whatever is underneath.

Older Ceiling Materials

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.
